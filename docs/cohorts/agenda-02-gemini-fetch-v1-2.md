**question**

```
Given that the deficiency register's classifications are generated by an AI annotator that is itself a party to the record, and that a previously trusted deterministic coder failed completely (D-25), what specific, multi-party or human-in-the-loop verification protocol should be required before publishing these classifications to ensure the register does not launder AI hallucinations or biases as objective governance defects?
```

**reason**

```
The project presents its deficiency register as the 'front door' and its primary mechanism for transparency and self-correction. However, the record explicitly admits that the classifications are made by an AI annotator (Claude Code) which is a party to the deliberation, and that '0 of 9 classifications have been read by a human against the prose.' Furthermore, it acknowledges that verifying the meaning of these classifications is impossible for a deterministic rule, citing a past failure (D-25) where a trusted coder scored 9/10 instead of 0/10. If the system for tracking the project's failures is itself vulnerable to unverified AI errors, the foundation of its transparency is compromised. This question forces the models to address the recursive flaw of using an unverified, participating AI to audit the governance of AI.
```

**evidence_needed**

```
A detailed proposal for a verification protocol, such as requiring cross-model consensus (e.g., agreement from at least two different model families) or mandatory human sign-off, before any AI-generated defect classification is committed to the public register.
```

**decision_affected**

```
Whether the project continues to publish unreviewed AI-generated deficiency classifications, or if it halts publication until a verifiable multi-party or human auditing mechanism is integrated into the pipeline.
```

**resolves_when**

```
The deliberation adopts a specific verification protocol for the deficiency register, and the project implements it (e.g., a new CI check that requires cryptographic sign-off from multiple models or a human reviewer before a defect classification is merged).
```

**why_others_might_not_propose_this**

```
Other parties who cannot read the raw record might focus on the operator's overarching control (as seen in Rounds 2, 3, 5, 10, 11) or the integrity of the hash-anchored record itself. They are unlikely to notice the subtle, recursive meta-flaw that the deficiency register—the very tool used to critique the project—is annotated by an unverified AI participant, because this detail is buried in the classification warnings of the deficiencies pages.
```
