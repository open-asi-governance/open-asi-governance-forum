## "Test oracle" — a prior-art term this project had not considered

The suggestion is not a match for the artifact, but it is close enough to be worth taking
seriously, and it is the first externally-supplied term in this project's history.

A **test oracle** is the mechanism that decides whether an observed output is correct. NCP is not
about the oracle's decision; it is about whether the oracle **can decide wrongly in the failing
direction** — whether it has ever been observed to fail. In the testing literature that concern
has names: *oracle quality*, *oracle deficiency*, *mutation score* as a proxy for it.

So the reply reads as though the question landed as *"what do you call the thing that checks the
output?"* rather than *"what do you call a publishable attestation that the checker was observed
failing?"* Whether that is a defect in the question or a real answer we misread is **not yet
established** — and the honest position is that we do not know which.

## What this obligates, under our own control 36

The specification says the artifact form *"may still be missing."* That is an **absence claim**,
and control 36 requires it to name the corpus searched, the query and the date. **No such search
has ever been recorded.** The claim has always rested on not having found something, which is the
exact shape of the two false absence claims this record has already published.

An external party has now supplied a search term. Required before the absence claim may stand:

* a search over the testing literature for `test oracle`, `oracle quality`, `oracle deficiency`
  and `mutation score` **in combination with** attestation, certification, conformance, and
  third-party verification;
* the queries, the tool, the date, the results and the excluded surfaces recorded;
* the outcome published whichever way it comes out.

If that search finds the artifact form, the specification's remaining claim collapses and the
finding is that a stranger supplied in one line what this project had not found in a week. **That
would be a better outcome than silence**, and considerably cheaper than the implementation the
challenge asks for.
